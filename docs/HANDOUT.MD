This gathering is for anyone curious about software freedom, open collaboration, privacy, digital rights, community-driven technology, or simply learning what these ideas mean. You do not need to be an expert, a developer, or already aligned with any one organization to be here.

Our goal is to create a space where people can learn, ask questions, share ideas, and build a recurring community around technology that serves people well.

## What “Libre” means

“Libre” points to freedom: the ability to run, study, share, and modify software. That idea is central to free software and strongly overlaps with open source.

In practice, many people use the terms differently:

- **Free software** emphasizes user freedom and ethics.
- **Open source** emphasizes practical collaboration, transparency, and broad adoption.
- **Libre** often bridges both ideas and is used as a welcoming umbrella term.

## Where they overlap

Even when people prefer different words, the shared values are often the same:

- Access to source code.
- The ability to learn from and improve software.
- Community participation.
- Better control over the tools we use.

The main difference is emphasis, not total opposition. Many people care about both freedom and practical usefulness at the same time.

## Why we are gathering

We are gathering because communities grow through repeated conversation, not one-off events.

This meetup can help us:

- Welcome people who are new to the topic.
- Explain the landscape without assuming prior knowledge.
- Share projects, tools, and ideas.
- Discover what kinds of future events would be valuable.
- Decide whether this should become a regular community space.

## FAQ

### Do I need a technical background?

No. Beginners are welcome.

### Is this only for FSF supporters?

No. People with different perspectives are welcome, including those who identify with free software, open source, privacy, or general tech curiosity.

### What if I do not know the terminology?

That is fine. The point of the meetup is to make the ideas understandable.

### Is there a “right” opinion here?

No single one. The goal is respectful discussion and shared learning.

### Where can I find the glossary?

See the [Lexicon](lexicon.md).

### Why make this a regular event?

Because recurring meetings make it easier to build trust, share progress, and turn ideas into action.

### What can I expect to leave with?

A better understanding of the terms, new connections, and a clearer sense of why these ideas matter.
